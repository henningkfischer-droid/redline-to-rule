Five steps

How it works

Rules come from what the reviewer actually changed, not from anyone's description of their preferences.

1

Compare and tag

1.1 Compare. Use your draft and the clean version that came back, one Word file with tracked changes, or your notes when the feedback was only oral. Every change is shown exactly.

1.2 Tag. Record why each change was made: a category, a one-line reason and a scope.

2

Ledger

Every tagged change in one searchable record: the evidence behind your rules.

3

Rules

3.1 Learn. Recurring tagged changes become candidate rules, each with its evidence attached.

3.2 Confirm. Accept or reject candidates. Only rules with enough evidence enter the skill.

4

Check

Test a new draft against the rules before it goes out: fixed text checks, plus an optional AI review of the rest.

5

Export

Compile the confirmed rules, each with an example, into a plain-text skill for any approved AI tool, or a Word or PDF rules sheet.

Step 1.1 · Compare

Compare drafts

Choose how the feedback came back. The comparison starts by itself as soon as both versions are in. Three ways in: your draft plus the reviewer's clean version (paste, .txt or .docx); one Word file with tracked changes, from which the app rebuilds both versions; or, when the reviewer only talked you through it, your notes of the oral feedback, one point per line. Text is compared paragraph by paragraph and word by word; spacing and blank lines are ignored. Each feedback point becomes a card you tag like an edit.

Step 1.2 · Tag

Tag the changes

Pick a category, write a one-line reason and press Enter. The next change opens by itself. Choose a category and write a one-line reason in your own words. Use "This matter only" for edits driven by the facts of one deal; those never count toward a rule. "Judgment call/one-off" is also excluded from rule counting.

Step 2 · Ledger

The ledger

A searchable record of every tagged change across all reviewers. Select a row to open it. The ledger is the evidence base. Candidate rules are mined from rows whose scope is "Always". Search looks at reasons, changed text, matters and categories.

Step 3.1 · Learn

Your rules

Candidates appear when the same kind of change recurs for this reviewer. Edit the wording, then confirm or reject. Changes are grouped by reviewer and category, then by similar reasons (shared key words; with AI on, also reasons the AI found to mean the same thing). Changes tagged "This matter only" or "Judgment call/one-off" are ignored. A rule can only be confirmed once it has at least the minimum number of instances.

Only confirmed rules with at least N instances enter the skill and the draft check.

Step 3.2 · Confirm

Confirmed rules

Each rule keeps its evidence. A rule is flagged "May be outdated" when newer evidence contradicts it.

Step 4 · Check

Check a draft

Paste a new draft. It is checked right away against the confirmed rules for this reviewer. Auto-checkable rules are tested with fixed text patterns, not AI, so read each result with care. Human-checked rules become a checklist; with AI on, you can also ask the AI to review them, and every quote it gives is checked against your draft. Only confirmed rules that meet the minimum instances are used.

About this project

    
Redline to Rule

    
Junior lawyers get the same edits from the same reviewer again and again. Redline to Rule turns those edits into written drafting rules, each backed by the changes that prove it, so the next draft gets them right the first time.

    

      
How rules are found

      
Every change you tag is kept as evidence. When the same kind of change recurs for a reviewer at least N times (set on the Rules screen; 2 by default), it becomes a candidate rule. Only rules a person confirms enter the skill and the draft check.

      
Optional AI

      
Off until you switch it on. It suggests tags, groups reasons that mean the same thing, proposes rule wording and reviews a draft. Every finding must quote the draft, and the app labels each quote Verbatim, Close or May be invented.

      
Privacy

      
Everything runs in this browser and is stored only here. With AI on, only the text an AI feature needs goes to OpenRouter; the page can contact no other address.
